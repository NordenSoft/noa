## What was closed

Nine forged-approval paths across five adversarial review rounds. Every one reproduced by the lead
before a line changed; every one has a regression test.

All nine are one class in five disguises: **a decision that depends on a replaceable part of the
environment is not a decision.** Rounds 1–3 each hardened the mechanisms they had NAMED and left the
class open in the same function. Round 4 found a member no round had modelled — a property WRITE,
where `[[Set]]` walks the prototype chain and no builtin is replaced at all. Round 5 found the round-4
fix had hardened one write target and left its neighbour four lines away.

**Fixing a site and not its neighbour produced a CRITICAL in three consecutive releases.** That is the
one thing to carry forward.

### 3. ADR-0006 — the decision that unblocks item 2

`packages/gate/src/wrapper.ts:135`:

```ts
execute: () => Promise<{ ok: boolean; detail?: string }>;
```

A zero-argument callback: the boundary cannot bind WHAT EXECUTED to WHAT WAS GRANTED. The gate names
the remedy itself — "it must accept a typed execution command it constructs itself" — which is a
breaking signature change, i.e. ADR-0006.

**The two failing tests are red BY DESIGN.** They assert `notStrictEqual` on values that are currently
equal; they are a specification held open until the decision lands, not a bug to be fixed.

Round 4's reviewer was explicit: split ADR-0006 into **0006-A** (reversible, stages 5–7) and **0006-B**
(ONE-WAY, stages 8–10) as SEPARATE decisions, *"never bundled — because bundling reversible work with
one-way commitments is exactly why ADR-0003 and ADR-0004 were rejected."*

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| **(a) decide ADR-0006, implement 0006-A** | **RECOMMENDED** — fixes the defect, clears the checks honestly |
| (b) add a bypass actor to the ruleset | permanently weakens the control that just proved it works |
| (c) drop the two checks from the required list | the same, and quieter |

The lead did not take this decision. Implementing it to unblock its own merge would be exactly the
move the reviewer warned against, in the one place this engagement has been most careful.

The argument for shipping was never "the code is clean" — the audit denies that in its own words. It

live and exploitable."**

Also open and unmeasured: ~270 flagged sites inside the site-gate budget; 37 property-write sites
outside any budget because the call/read grammar cannot express a write; the new verdict-differential
gate's oracle list is hand-written and unreconciled, so an undeclared verdict-bearing export is
invisible to it. Each is named in the audit.

## Two lead errors, recorded because the next reader will repeat them

**Wrong query, confident conclusion.** The lead queried repository-level CodeQL alerts, got `0 open`,
and wrote into a merge-commit message that CodeQL was "not a finding". It was the wrong endpoint. With
`--admin` in hand, five unexamined HIGH alerts were one command from being overridden. GitHub's
refusal is the only reason it surfaced.

**Seven hand-built probes failed, every one caught by its control.** One CRITICAL was nearly recorded
as "not reproduced" because the probe died at a hash check before reaching the code under test. Shipped
attack tests now assert the refusal REASON, so a probe that dies early fails loudly instead of
certifying nothing. **That is the whole argument for shipping a control with every attack.**
